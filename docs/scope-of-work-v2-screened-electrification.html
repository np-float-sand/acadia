Background#

The Consultant has designed and tested a quantitative equity strategy — Screened Electrification Equipment — that invests in the companies supplying the equipment and construction services for the U.S. electricity grid build-out. The thesis rests on physical need (AI data-center load growth, electrification of heating and transport, and reshoring of domestic manufacturing) rather than investor sentiment. A fixed, rules-based screen selects profitable, reasonably-valued equipment and construction suppliers — currently 27 names, with an adopted extension to 29 (see Phase 1) — and the resulting book is managed with a set of automatic risk overlays: a gold/bond macro reserve, a valuation-extension de-risk ladder, and volatility targeting. Two variants are offered: Option A (buy-only) and Option B (Option A plus a hedge against a broad U.S. industrials basket). Full construction detail, performance, and known limitations are documented in docs/electrification-strategy-proposal-v6.md, the Phase 2 findings report referenced throughout this Scope of Work.

This document supersedes the original draft's framing around a grid-resilience "stress beta" long/short book (an 18-utility strategy that was explored in earlier research but not carried into delivery). The Phase 1–3 scope below describes the work actually performed, and remaining, against Screened Electrification Equipment.

The Client will provide access to risk model data. The Consultant will source all other required data from public and commercially available sources as described herein.

Scope of Work#

Phase 1 — Data Integration & Signal Development#

Estimated: 50–70 hours

The Consultant will:

  1. Establish and validate the data pipeline; integrate short interest. Consolidated the existing equity-price, fundamental, and macro (FRED) data pipeline into a reproducible, cached research environment, confirmed to rebuild cleanly from a cold cache. Closed a real gap by adding short-interest tracking (short_interest.py): a live yfinance snapshot of DLR/EQIX short interest captured on every pipeline run. No free historical short-interest endpoint exists, so this accumulates history going forward rather than backfilling; it is used as a cost/liquidity diagnostic for the strategy's hedge short leg, not as a signal input.

  2. Forward power price data — descoped. The shipped strategy does not require a forward-looking power-price input. Feasibility work on two candidate sources (the EIA STEO ELWHU_* forward series and the ERCOT real-time/day-ahead LMP spread) is complete and documented as available future work, not exercised further. See docs/RESEARCH-LOG.md §4 item 20, docs/steo-archive-feasibility-results.md, and docs/rt-da-spread-feasibility-results.md.

  3. Integrate Client-provided risk data. Built risk_exposures.py, an adapter against a placeholder schema (long-format date, ticker, factor, exposure), tested independent of real data, with a documented swap-in point for the Client's risk-model file. No file has been received from the Client as of this writing — this remains genuinely pending on the Client, not a gap in delivery.

  4. Develop enhanced signal component — stress beta. A pre-registered, run-once statistical test — never previously conducted on this supplier universe — examined whether the strategy's sensitivity to grid-stress events (reusing the Grid Stress Index and conditional-beta methodology built for the earlier grid-resilience research) could be turned into a scoring signal. The pre-registered gate required a pooled t-statistic of at least 2.0 on the 2021-present window; the observed result was t = 0.144, and the gate did not pass. Per the pre-registered protocol, the signal was not wired into the strategy. In its place, this deliverable formalizes the three risk-management rules already built into the strategy — the 15% GLD/IEF macro reserve, the valuation-extension de-risk ladder, and 20%-target volatility targeting — as the practical, evidence-backed answer to grid-stress sensitivity, each mapped to a specific stress category and supported by an episode-drawdown table across five real market shocks (COVID, the 2022 rate shock, the DeepSeek sell-off, the 2025 tariff shock, and the 2026 sell-off). Full methodology and results: docs/electrification-stress-beta-gate-results.md and docs/electrification-stress-sensitivity-response.md.

  5. Additional, beyond the original scope: REIT-breadth universe extension. A probe testing whether adding two data-center-exposed REITs (IRM, AMT) to the supplier universe improves the strategy was run under a pre-registered gate. Result: adopted. The resulting 29-name screen_reit construction beats the base 27-name screen on both Sharpe and maximum drawdown in every overlay stack tested, and is retained as an available, documented construction. See docs/electrification-reit-breadth-probe-results.md.

Deliverable: reproducible data pipeline with short-interest and risk-exposure diagnostics; pre-registered stress-beta gate results and the resulting stress-sensitivity response; an adopted REIT-breadth universe extension.

Phase 2 — Backtest & Validation#

Estimated: 40–60 hours

The Consultant will:

  1. Create Strategy — extended backtest.py/__main__.py to wire Phase 1's risk-exposure and short-interest diagnostics into the output bundle as reporting, not as hard portfolio constraints, since no real Client risk data exists yet to optimize against.
  2. Run rolling backtest — re-ran the full backtest end to end from a genuinely cold cache and confirmed it is reproducible (515 tests passing; clean output bundle of metrics.csv, episode_drawdowns.csv, plateau.json, returns.csv, and performance.png).
  3. Produce written findings report — docs/electrification-strategy-proposal-v6.md (with accompanying HTML/PDF), summarizing performance results, signal construction, the stress-beta gate outcome, the short-interest and risk-data integration status, known limitations, and the recommended configuration.

Deliverable: backtest results (code + output files), written findings report (docs/electrification-strategy-proposal-v6.md).

Phase 3 — Handoff & Documentation#

Estimated: 20–30 hours

Items 1, 2 and the manifest portion of item 4 are complete and included in this tagged release — docs/technical-reference.md (code documentation), docs/runbook.md (operational runbook), and docs/deliverable-manifest.md (ships-vs-internal manifest). Item 3 (handoff sessions) and the final tagged code delivery in item 4 follow Client review of the Phase 2 findings report and depend on Client scheduling. The Consultant will:

  1. Document all code — provide inline documentation and function-level docstrings across electrification_strategy/, including the short-interest and risk-exposure modules added in Phase 1, and a technical reference (docs/technical-reference.md) covering each module's inputs, outputs, and role in the pipeline — including the placeholder risk-data schema contract for the Client's future risk-model file.

  2. Write operational runbook — docs/runbook.md: step-by-step instructions for environment setup, refreshing data caches, running the pipeline, rebalancing, interpreting output, and handling common failure modes.

  3. Conduct handoff sessions — participate in working sessions with the Client's designated technical contact to walk through the codebase, answer questions, and confirm the Client team can operate and extend the system independently. Scheduling depends on Client availability.

  4. Deliver final codebase — provide a deliverable manifest (docs/deliverable-manifest.md) listing exactly what ships versus what remains internal research, followed by a clean, tagged release of the full repository in a format specified by the Client.

Deliverable: documented codebase, runbook, handoff session(s), final code delivery.

The Client will provide data from commercial risk model. All data sourced by the Consultant is drawn from public or commercially available sources. The Consultant will not use any material non-public information in the course of this work.

Intellectual Property#

Upon receipt of full payment for each phase, the Consultant assigns to the Client all right, title, and interest in the work product produced under that phase, including source code, documentation, backtest output, and written findings. The Client will own all deliverables outright and may use, modify, and extend them without restriction.

The Consultant retains no rights to the deliverables and will not use Client-provided data for any purpose outside this engagement.

Scope Limitations#

The following are explicitly outside the scope of this engagement:

  • Live trading or portfolio management. The Consultant's role is limited to historical research and backtesting. All trading decisions are made exclusively by the Client.
  • Investment advice. Nothing in this engagement constitutes investment advice or a recommendation to buy or sell any security.
  • Access to current or live positions. The Consultant will work with historical and publicly available data only and will not have access to the Client's current portfolio, positions, or live trading systems.
  • Performance guarantees. Backtest results are historical simulations. Past simulated performance is not indicative of future results. No representation is made regarding live performance.

Term#

This engagement begins upon execution and continues until completion of Phase 3 or mutual written agreement to terminate, whichever comes first. Either party may terminate with 30 days written notice. Upon termination, the Client will owe fees for all hours worked and data costs incurred through the notice period, and the Consultant will deliver all work product completed to that point.
