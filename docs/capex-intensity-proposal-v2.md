# Capex-Intensity Long/Short

**Strategy summary — v2, 22 September 2026**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Classification** | Long/short equity, sector-focused (US power & electrification) |
| **Objective** | Capture the electricity build-out through the firms that supply it, funded partly by shorting the firms that finance it |
| **Universe** | ~150–190 US-listed issuers, defined mechanically from SEC filings |
| **Selection** | Trailing-twelve-month capital expenditure ÷ revenue, point-in-time |
| **Book** | Long lightest quartile · short heaviest quartile at 0.5× · ~22 long / 22 short |
| **Rebalance** | Monthly · turnover ≈ 1.0× per annum |
| **Risk control** | 20% annualised volatility target, leverage capped at 1.5× |
| **Status** | Implemented (`capex_intensity_strategy/`, 18 tests). **Not live.** |

---

## 1. Investment objective

The strategy seeks total return by holding companies whose revenues are generated by
supplying the electricity build-out, while shorting companies whose economics require
funding that build-out from their own balance sheets.

It is a **sector-relative** strategy. It does not seek to time the build-out, and it
does not hedge broad equity risk.

## 2. Investment thesis

Electricity demand in the United States has inflected after two decades of flat
consumption. Meeting it requires capital: generation, transmission, substations,
switchgear, and the construction labour to install all of it.

That capital is raised and spent by one set of companies and earned by another.

> **Utilities, pipelines and miners *fund* the build-out and earn a regulated or
> commodity return on the assets they carry. Equipment manufacturers, electrical
> contractors and fuel-cycle suppliers *are paid by* the build-out and carry
> comparatively little of it on their own balance sheets.**

Both groups are exposed to the same underlying activity. Only one converts it into
revenue without simultaneously converting it into depreciation, rate-base risk and
financing need. Capital expenditure as a proportion of revenue separates them, and does
so from data every issuer is required to file.

## 3. Universe construction

Membership is determined mechanically. No issuer is selected by hand, and no list is
maintained by the manager.

### 3.1 Structural screen — SEC industry classification

All US issuers filing an annual report under a defined set of SEC Standard Industrial
Classification codes are candidates: electrical work, switchgear and transformers,
motors and generators, industrial and electrical machinery, measuring instruments,
electrical wholesale, **power-line construction**, electric services, electric and gas
utilities, cogeneration, gas transmission and distribution, and coal.

SIC codes are assigned by the filer at the time of filing and are therefore available
contemporaneously.

**Three classifications are deliberately excluded:**

| excluded | reason |
|---|---|
| 3674 — Semiconductors | 505 filers; would introduce AMD, Broadcom and ARM, which are not electrification businesses |
| 1311 — Crude petroleum & natural gas | ~900 filers, predominantly exploration and production |
| 1040 — Metal mining | 523 filers, predominantly gold and silver |

### 3.2 Supplemental screen — fuel-cycle lexicon

A defined set of phrases is searched against annual reports filed in the two years
preceding each universe year:

> *"uranium concentrates" · "nuclear fuel cycle" · "uranium enrichment" · "yellowcake" ·
> "enriched uranium"*

Issuers matching any phrase join the universe from that year forward.

**Why this screen exists.** It is not a thematic overlay. It corrects one specific,
identified defect in the structural screen: **nuclear fuel-cycle companies do not carry
an energy SIC code.** Cameco and Uranium Energy file under 1090 (Miscellaneous Metal
Ores); Centrus and Energy Fuels file under 1400 (Nonmetallic Minerals). Both codes are
populated overwhelmingly by gold and silver miners, so no SIC-based filter can isolate
the fuel cycle without admitting several hundred unrelated issuers.

Of eight fuel-cycle issuers tested, **none** was reachable structurally. The lexicon
recovers all of them, together with peers not otherwise identified — Denison, NexGen,
Ur-Energy, enCore, ASP Isotopes, Lightbridge.

**Why this screen is limited to the fuel cycle.** Seven further lexicons were tested
against the same standard: *does the vocabulary recover issuers the structural screen
cannot reach, without admitting unrelated ones?*

| lexicon | listed matches | new vs SIC | what the new names actually were |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nuclear fuel cycle** | 42 | **16** | **Cameco, Centrus, Denison, NexGen, enCore, Ur-Energy — genuine fuel-cycle issuers** |
| Battery storage | 94 | 53 | Albemarle, BHP, and unrelated micro-capitalisation issuers |
| Hydrogen | 66 | 46 | BP, Chevron, Air Products, CF Industries — integrated energy and chemicals |
| Solar | 52 | 37 | Applied Materials, CleanSpark, and other non-solar issuers |
| Wind | 34 | 24 | Eni, Equinor, Enbridge — integrated oil and gas |
| Marine / tidal | 9 | 6 | Eni, Ecopetrol, TechnipFMC, Oceaneering — oilfield services |
| Geothermal | 6 | 5 | Newmont, Schlumberger |
| Transmission services | 4 | 1 | MDU Resources |

The pattern is consistent and explains the design. **Nuclear fuel-cycle vocabulary is a
term of art.** An issuer writes "yellowcake" or "uranium concentrates" only if it
handles the material. By contrast "green hydrogen", "offshore wind" and "tidal energy"
appear routinely in the sustainability disclosures of integrated oil companies that have
no operating exposure to them. A lexicon is only a valid instrument where the vocabulary
is exclusive to practitioners.

**Why other renewable verticals need no lexicon.** They are already reachable:

| vertical | structural coverage |
|---|---|
| Battery storage | Fluence, Eos, Enovix, Amprius — all SIC 3690 |
| Hydrogen & fuel cells | Plug, Bloom, FuelCell, Ballard — all SIC 3620 |
| Geothermal | Ormat — SIC 4911 |
| **Nuclear fuel cycle** | **none — the gap the lexicon fills** |

### 3.3 Liquidity

Trailing one-year median daily dollar volume must exceed $10 million, tested monthly.
Issuers enter and leave as liquidity changes.

Resulting universe: 100 issuers (2019), 131 (2023), 159 (2025).

## 4. Selection methodology

For each issuer and each month-end, the strategy computes **trailing-twelve-month
capital expenditure divided by trailing-twelve-month revenue** from XBRL data in SEC
filings.

Point-in-time integrity is enforced at four points:

1. Each fact is keyed on the date it was **filed**, never on the period it describes. A
   quarter is invisible until the day it reaches the SEC.
2. Only single-quarter flow facts (spans of 60–200 days) are used, so a trailing sum
   never double-counts an annual or year-to-date figure.
3. Restatements resolve to the most recently filed value **as at the observation date**.
4. An issuer requires four filed quarters before it is scored; no partial year is
   annualised.

## 5. Portfolio construction

| parameter | value |
|---|---|
| Long book | lightest quartile by capex intensity, equally weighted |
| Short book | heaviest quartile, equally weighted, scaled to **0.5×** the long book |
| Rebalance | monthly |
| Transaction costs | 10 basis points on traded notional |
| Volatility target | 20% annualised, 42-day trailing, leverage capped at 1.5× |

The book is **net long by design.** The short leg funds and dampens rather than
neutralises. Short weight was examined across its range; return per unit of risk rises
monotonically to 0.5× and declines beyond it.

### 6.1 Attribution of the design choices

| variant | 2023–26 Sharpe | 2025–26 Sharpe |
|---|---|---|
| **Nuclear lexicon (as specified)** | **1.23** | **1.12** |
| Lexicon widened to solar | 0.90 | 1.01 |
| Lexicon widened to wind | 1.23 | 1.12 |
| Short leg removed (long only) | 1.02 | 0.96 |
| Utilities excluded from the universe | 1.10 | 1.05 |

Widening the lexicon to solar **reduces** risk-adjusted return by roughly a third. The
reason is instructive: Array Technologies and Nextracker are genuinely asset-light and
therefore rank as long candidates, but their economics depend on subsidy and they
exhibit little pricing power. **Capital intensity measures where capital sits; it does
not measure whether revenue is durable.** Widening to wind changes nothing, as no
US-listed pure-play wind manufacturer clears the liquidity floor.

## 8. Risk factors

**8.1 Cycle dependence.** Returns are earned while the capital cycle runs. In-sample
Sharpe was 0.41. *Stated exit condition: should the return spread between the light and
heavy capital-intensity baskets remain flat over four consecutive quarters, the strategy
should be stood down rather than held through a drawdown.*

**8.2 Metric selection risk.** Eleven candidate metrics were evaluated against a
classified reference list. Capital intensity was selected on a t-statistic of −15.1
against −7.2 for the next strongest. The rule requires no reference list once specified,
and the results above do not use one — but the *choice* of metric was informed by
outcomes, and only forward observation resolves this.

**8.3 Factor overlap.** Low capital intensity is related to documented quality and
capital-discipline factors. The strategy is a sector-specific application, not an
independent anomaly.

**8.4 Short-side borrow.** Regulated utilities are readily borrowable. Several smaller
short-book constituents are not. The short book should be assumed partially unfillable.

**8.5 Concentration.** Approximately 22 positions per side in a single sector.

## 9. Limitations and disclosures

**9.1 Survivorship.** Price data is sourced from a provider that does not carry delisted
securities. Of 2,535 filers in the relevant classifications, 2,168 have no current
listing. The long book is therefore flattered by the absence of failures, and the short
book understated, since bankruptcies would have been profitable short positions. **This
affects every backtest in this repository, including the shipped strategy.** Resolution
requires a survivorship-free price history.

**9.2 Not live.** No capital has been allocated. Results are simulated.

**9.3 Pre-live conditions.** (i) date-stamp and freeze the specification; (ii) obtain
survivorship-free pricing and re-run; (iii) test the metric against a general
asset-light factor outside this sector; (iv) borrow and capacity study; (v) determine
sizing relative to the existing Screened Electrification book, with which correlation
is 0.78.
