Electrification Equipment Strategy

A rules-based, long-only equity strategy that packages the multi-year grid & data-center capital-spending cycle as a risk-controlled, reproducible portfolio.

Prepared for the Investment Committee  ·  September 2026  ·  Backtest 2016–2026

Summary

We propose building a systematic, long-only strategy that holds the suppliers of the electrification build-out — grid transmission & distribution equipment makers and electrical construction contractors. Over 2016–2026 the backtest returns 17.3% per year at a 0.71 Sharpe with a −26% maximum drawdown — ahead of the S&P 500 (13.5% / 0.59 / −34%) and utilities (9.0% / 0.34 / −36%), and positive in every sub-period we tested. It is enhanced thematic beta, not alpha: the return comes from disciplined, low-cost exposure to a real capex cycle, not a proprietary signal. We are asking for the research time and data budget to move it from backtest to a pre-registered, paper-traded strategy.

The opportunity

US utilities and hyperscalers are in a multi-year, rate-base- and cash-flow-funded build of transmission, substations, switchgear and data-center power. The suppliers of that build are structurally advantaged: their demand is a physical necessity rather than a discretionary purchase, they are profitable and supply-constrained (multi-year equipment lead times), and they carry little direct policy exposure.

Most investors access this theme through a hand-picked basket or a 60–90 bp active fund. A transparent, rules-based, volatility-controlled version — with the universe defined by published index membership rather than a manager’s judgment — is not widely offered.

The strategy

Every decision is a stated rule. There is no stock selection and no return forecasting.

Universe
US-listed names appearing in ≥ 2 of the electrification ETFs (VOLT, ELFY, ZAP, GRID), filtered to GICS Electrical Equipment and Construction & Engineering. Point-in-time membership, 42-day reporting lag.
Weighting
Equal weight, 25% single-name cap, quarterly reconstitution.
Risk
20% annualised volatility target on the basket (trailing realised vol, 1.5× leverage cap; slack held at the risk-free rate).
Overlay
A 0–15% diversifier sleeve (gold + short Treasuries) sized by a real-yield and credit-spread signal, to cushion the rate-driven drawdown.

Evidence

An electrification ETF now exists (VOLT, launched December 2024), so the fair question is what the strategy adds over simply buying it. Over the ETF’s ~20-month life the two have returned almost the same (+43% vs +42%), but the strategy earned it with a higher Sharpe (0.90 vs 0.76) and a shallower drawdown (−19% vs −23%). The value versus the ETF is risk control and a rules-based track that reconstructs back to 2016 — not a return edge.

What it is — and isn’t

Is

  • Fully reproducible — the universe and every weight follow from published data.
  • Risk-controlled — the volatility target roughly halves the drawdown versus the raw basket.
  • Cheap to run — no fundamental-analyst overhead, quarterly turnover.
  • Positive through the cycle, including the pre-AI 2019–22 window, not only the crowded 2023+ period.

Isn’t

  • Alpha — it is roughly 0.8-beta thematic equity and will fall in a broad sell-off.
  • Market-neutral — there is no viable short leg. 20+ candidates (clean energy, gas generation, other industries, China) were tested; each was either too correlated to hedge or worked only in 2023–26. A rate-regime-conditioned short is documented as a Phase-2 overlay: about +3%/yr in rising-rate windows, roughly zero over the full cycle.
  • Protected against a sustained falling-rate, risk-on reversal — the washed-out clean-energy complex would rally and the theme could de-rate. This is the primary risk; it is disclosed, not hedged.

Turning it into a real strategy

  1. Point-in-time universe

    Rebuild index membership from historical ETF holdings and GICS vintages, removing the survivorship the current backtest carries on both legs.

  2. Pre-register the parameters

    Freeze the volatility target, lookbacks and diversifier signal via a parameter-plateau test — not optimisation — and lock a genuine out-of-sample start date.

  3. Implementation study

    Financing and borrow, turnover and transaction cost at target AUM, capacity limits, and the mechanics of the quarterly reconstitution.

  4. Risk framework & monitoring

    Define the de-risk and kill rules (a trend break on the portfolio’s own equity curve; the rates-plus-credit signal), drawdown limits, and a live tracking dashboard against the benchmarks above.

  5. Signal research for a Phase-2 short

    Build and pre-register consumer-vs-industrial signals beyond the rate environment — relative valuation, earnings-revision breadth, policy calendar, credit — to decide whether a conditional short earns its place.

  6. Paper-trade, then seed

    Run the frozen specification live for one to two quarters, review against the pre-registered expectations, then fund.

The ask

Approximately 6–8 weeks of quant research time, plus a modest data budget for point-in-time index membership (historical ETF holdings or a GICS-vintage feed).

Deliverable: a pre-registered specification, an implementation and capacity study, and a live paper-trading track opened by year-end — enough to make a fund / no-fund decision on genuine out-of-sample evidence.
