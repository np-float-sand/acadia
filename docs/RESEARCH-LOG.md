## 2. Accumulated conclusion

**ROOT CAUSE IDENTIFIED (2026-09-21).** After ~42 attempts the failures now have a single
structural explanation rather than 42 separate ones: **energy futures markets efficiently price
public power/gas fundamentals, and power-levered equities price off those futures — so public,
aggregate power-market data cannot predict power equities, because the information is already
impounded one layer upstream.** Decisive test: natural-gas storage deviation from the 5-year
norm (the most-watched fundamental in the US gas market) does **not** predict forward Henry Hub
returns — t = +0.28/+0.70/−0.05 (21d), +0.10/+0.58/+0.97 (63d), +0.51/+0.80/+2.42 (126d) across
2010-18 / 2019-22 / 2023-26; one marginal hit in nine cells. If the most-watched gas fundamental
cannot predict gas prices, no gas/power fundamental can predict gas/power equities through the
price channel. This predicts that every signal built from national/hub-level EIA or ISO series
will fail — which is what this log records. **Implication for future work: stop testing aggregate
public series; look only where no futures market prices the information** (nodal basis for a
named fleet, plant-level generation by operator, or volatility rather than direction).
See `docs/power-market-signal-root-cause-results.md`.

**All three of those extensions were then built and tested the same day** (ledger rows below;
`docs/power-market-extensions-results.md`). Ext 1 (nodal) and Ext 3 (volatility) **FAILED**, both
partly on data availability — free ERCOT resource-node history is a ~30-day rolling window, and no
historical implied-vol feed is wired, so the variance-risk-premium idea is still *untested* rather
than refuted. Ext 2 produced the one genuinely new idea in this line: **for merchant generators,
physical output predicts the earnings reaction while EPS surprise does not** (EPS surprise →
announcement return is t=1.17, **R²=0.016**). That means **Probe A's 2026-09-07 failure was a
wrong-dependent-variable problem, not a dead mechanism** — it tested operating income, which
hedge MtM severs, when the market is reacting to volumes. The nowcast itself is watch-list only
(fails the 2018-22 regime split at t=0.22, and collapses to t=0.59 excluding NRG), but the
reframe should inform any future merchant-generator work: **test against output/volume and the
announcement reaction, not EPS.**

**Scope limit, established the same day (do not over-read the above).** The obvious cash-in —
adding a generation-volume feature to the already-built **Dispatch** L/S — was tested and
**FAILED**. Generation volume has no monthly cross-sectional power (ALL-23 IC +0.019, t=0.68), and
on the merchant subset it is **wrong-signed** versus the event-window result (IC −0.106, 2023-26);
adding it to the tercile book hurts full-sample (Sharpe 1.06→0.82) and 2025-26 (0.45→0.26).
Ext 2's effect is an **event-window** phenomenon — ~3 days, 4x/yr, when output is actually
revealed — and dissolves when diluted across all months. Both results hold simultaneously; this is
a scope limitation, not a contradiction. **Correct reading: "use output, not EPS" applies to
event-window studies of merchant generators. Generation volume is NOT a monthly factor.**

Across **~42 signal attempts** (this project + predecessors), **no tradable timing or
cross-sectional edge has survived an honest out-of-sample / regime split.** (Latest,
2026-09-16: a multi-hub ERCOT/PJM electricity-price-variance scan against crypto-miner and
merchant-generator equity variance, replicating a paywalled paper's claimed mechanism with a
mandatory BTC/SPY confound control — failed on both the miner and the parallel generator arm;
`docs/electricity-price-volatility-miner-results.md`.) Three independent
demand-data classes — forecast (PJM load forecasts), physical-flow (congestion, FTR), and
commitment (utility capex guidance) — have each failed. A fourth framing — **electricity as a
company-specific economic input** (physical power-cost/-revenue exposure → company earnings →
equity mispricing), the "move one layer closer to the market" pivot — also failed its own
pre-registered gate 1 (2026-09-07): a bottom-up fleet×price model tracks merchant-generator
*revenue* but not *operating income* (hedges + non-gen segments sever the link), and a
hand-mapped power-cost-exposure signal across ~18 power-intensive industrials leaves no
fingerprint on gross margin and no factor-neutralised forward-return predictability. The defensible product is the
**rules-based long-only book + risk overlays**, pitched as disciplined thematic beta, plus the
**`capex-cycle-pair-classifier` construction rule** (the one constructive output: it mechanically
reproduces the hand-picked book, removing the "you hand-picked winners" objection).

**Constructive exception found 2026-09-21 — the capex-intensity rule.** The one construction in
this line that is *fully ex-ante* (mechanical EDGAR-SIC + full-text universe, point-in-time TTM
capex/revenue score, no hand-picked membership and no 2026-dated flags) and clears a Sharpe bar in
both recent windows: **2023-26 Sharpe 1.26 / Calmar 1.39, 2025-26 Sharpe 1.07 / Calmar 1.23**, with
2023+ genuinely out-of-sample for the discovery. It is also the first book here whose **algorithmic
short leg improves Sharpe monotonically** rather than needing to be shrunk. It is *not* a
power-market timing signal — it is a correctly-specified cross-sectional factor (asset-light
suppliers vs asset-heavy owners) applied to a mechanically-defined power/electrification universe,
and it is weak pre-2023. It does not overturn the conclusion above about *timing* signals; it does
show that the universe-and-selection problem was solvable where the timing problem was not.
`docs/capex-intensity-rule-results.md`.

**One partial exception, watch-list only (2026-09-08):** the narrative-mispricing thread's
follow-on — a GBM over the *combination* of merchant stress-beta + ICR + EDGAR disclosure counts
+ momentum — is the first attempt in the ~28 to clear a shuffled-label null cleanly and survive a
merchant-confound exclusion (Fold B, train 2024-25 / test 2026, OOS AUC 0.758). It still does
**not** survive a regime split (flat pre-2023) and rests on 6 months / ~50 observations, so it is
**not tradable** — logged as §4 item 18 to re-check quarterly, not a change to the conclusion above.
`docs/handoff-narrative-mispricing-portal.md`. The **returns** expression of that model was then
built and tested to a verdict (2026-09-08 → 09-09, "**Dispatch**", `docs/handoff-2026-09-08-dispatch-ls.md`):
as a dollar-neutral cross-sectional L/S it **FAILED the regime split** — even after pruning the
dead features (`gdelt_z`, stale `icr_z`) and widening the universe 13 → 19 names, ~100% of the
Sharpe is the 2024 merchant-vs-regulated melt-up (ex-2024 Sharpe −0.05), and a sector-neutral /
regulated-only cut leaves only a trivial non-fundable signal. So the watch-list exception is
**narrower than it looked**: it survives only as a tercile-*classification*-AUC curiosity on
2026 (§4 item 18, re-check quarterly), not as a tradable book. The one durable output is the
**point-in-time trailing-net-margin screen**, kept as a quality overlay (it lifted the worst
year of every Dispatch variant and of the shipped long-only book).

**Selection-alpha probe (2026-09-24).** The shipped book's GRID-hedged selection Sharpe is 0.09, so
a direct search was run for a within-universe ranking signal (14 signals, ~146 names/mo, bar
\|t\|≥1.5 pooled and both halves). **None passes.** Energy/congestion/scarcity prices do not rank
names (exposure×shock flat; exposure levels = the 2023-26 theme). The only information found is
fundamental: **capex intensity** (predicts in the *heavy* tail — an avoid/short signal, the same
place Part B's un-shippable short sits) and **revenue acceleration** (top tail). Their post-hoc
composite is the best long-side selector yet (GRID-hedged 0.42) and is a lead to be frozen and
tested out-of-sample, not a result. `docs/xsec-alpha-probe-results.md`.
