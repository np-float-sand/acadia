Capex-Intensity Long/Short — Research Details

Internal. Full specification, evidence, traps and known weaknesses. Not for distribution. Version 22 September 2026 · implemented in capex_intensity_strategy/ · not live.

1. The rule, in full

Universe (mechanical, point-in-time).

  1. Structural screen. Every US 10-K filer whose SEC SIC code is in the electrical/power set: 1731 electrical work · 3470 coating · 3490 fabricated metal · 3510 engines & turbines · 3550 special industry machinery · 3561 pumps · 3569 general industrial machinery · 3585 HVAC · 3590 misc industrial · 3600 electronic & electrical equipment · 3612 transformers · 3613 switchgear · 3620 electrical apparatus · 3621 motors & generators · 3670 electronic components · 3679 components NEC · 3690 misc electrical · 3823/3829 instruments · 5063 electrical wholesale · 1623 power-line construction · 4911 electric services · 4922/4923/ 4924 gas transmission & distribution · 4931 electric & other combined · 4991 cogeneration · 1221 coal. Excluded deliberately: 3674 semiconductors (505 filers; admits AMD/AVGO/ARM), 1311 crude oil & gas (~900), 1040 metal mining (523, mostly gold).
  2. Lexicon screen. Any filer whose 10-K/40-F/20-F in the two preceding years contains "uranium concentrates", "nuclear fuel cycle", "uranium enrichment", "yellowcake" or "enriched uranium". Effective from that universe-year forward.
  3. Liquidity. Trailing 1-year median dollar volume > $10M, re-tested monthly.

Score. Point-in-time TTM capital expenditure ÷ TTM revenue, from EDGAR companyfacts, keyed on each fact's filed date. Only single-quarter flow facts (60–200 day spans). Four filed quarters required. Restatements resolve to latest-filed-as-of-date.

Book. Long lightest quartile equal-weighted; short heaviest quartile at 0.5×; monthly rebalance; 10bp costs; 20% vol target, leverage cap 1.5×.

3. Leg decomposition — where the return actually comes from

leg 2023–26 Sharpe 2025–26 Sharpe 2019–26 Sharpe 2019–26 CAGR
Long — asset-light suppliers 1.12 1.09 0.75 +23.3%
Short — as held (capital-heavy) −0.42 −0.40 −0.45 −8.2%
Short basket viewed long-only −0.02 −0.03 +0.08 +3.4%
Combined 1.0L − 0.5S 1.24 1.24 0.85 +21.5%

The short leg loses money standalone and still improves the book — Sharpe 1.12 → 1.24. It is a hedge, not an alpha source. A position improves portfolio Sharpe when its own Sharpe exceeds correlation × portfolio Sharpe, which is a much lower bar than zero. This is the same arithmetic recorded in docs/RESEARCH-LOG.md (interconnection-queue thread).

Practical consequence: do not market the short leg as a return driver, and do not size it as one. Past 0.5× it degrades the book.

4. Interest-rate sensitivity

measure result
Beta to daily change in 10y real yield −0.0001, t = −0.03 — rate-neutral
High real-yield regime (above expanding median) Sharpe 0.95, CAGR 24.4%
Low real-yield regime Sharpe 0.58, CAGR 14.9%
Real yields rising (3m change > 0), 2025–26 Sharpe 1.57
Real yields falling, 2025–26 Sharpe 0.60

It has no measurable duration beta, but it is structurally long the cost of capital. The short leg is capital-hungry, long-duration businesses — utilities, pipelines, developers — whose valuations and economics improve when capital is cheap. Expensive capital punishes them and rewards the asset-light long leg.

Weak environment: a sustained low or falling real-rate regime (Sharpe 0.58 vs 0.95). That is the mirror image of the shipped Screened Electrification book, whose disclosed primary risk is also a falling-rate reversal — so the two books share this exposure rather than offsetting it.

5. Does the short leg help the shipped book?

Yes — materially, and more than it helps its own book.

base + capex short 2023–26 Sh / Cal 2025–26 Sh / Cal
screen +val+sleeve 0.00× 1.56 / 1.82 1.30 / 1.57
0.25× 1.67 / 2.02 1.41 / 1.74
0.50× 1.62 / 2.08 1.40 / 1.79
screen +val+sleeve+short 0.00× 1.54 / 2.31 1.46 / 2.32
0.25× 1.59 / 2.66 1.56 / 2.60
0.50× 1.47 / 2.69 1.48 / 2.64

A 0.25× capex-intensity short sleeve improves the shipped book on both metrics in both windows — the best single result produced in this line of work (2025–26 Sharpe 1.56, Calmar 2.60). It also stacks with the existing DLR/EQIX short rather than replacing it: the two hedge different things (capital intensity vs rate-sensitive duration).

This is arguably the most commercially useful finding here — it improves an already-shipped book without changing its long leg.

6. Does capital intensity work outside power?

universe names 2023–26 Sh / Cal 2025–26 Sh / Cal 2019–26 Sh
Power / electrification ~150–190 1.23 / 1.40 1.12 / 1.30 0.80
Non-power industrials (7 themes) 74 0.79 / 1.20 0.46 / 0.79 0.52
— semicap 13 0.11 / 0.10 0.39 / 0.55 0.26
— reshoring / E&C 14 0.48 / 0.48 0.71 / 1.21 0.46

The factor is not power-exclusive, but power is where it works. Outside power it is positive and roughly half to a third as strong. Two readings, and I cannot separate them with this evidence:

  • Economic: power is where the capital cycle is currently most extreme, so the supplier/owner distinction bites hardest.
  • Statistical: the non-power test has 74 names against 150–190 and a shorter effective history, so it is a weaker test rather than a weaker effect.

Implication: treat it as a sector-specific application. Do not market it as a general equity factor without running the broad test properly.

7. Lexicon design — why nuclear only

Seven alternative lexicons were tested against one standard: does the vocabulary recover issuers the SIC screen cannot reach, without admitting unrelated ones?

lexicon listed new vs SIC what the new names were
Nuclear fuel cycle 42 16 Cameco, Centrus, Denison, NexGen, enCore, Ur-Energy
Battery storage 94 53 Albemarle, BHP, micro-caps
Hydrogen 66 46 BP, Chevron, Air Products, CF
Solar 52 37 Applied Materials, CleanSpark
Wind 34 24 Eni, Equinor, Enbridge
Marine / tidal 9 6 TechnipFMC, Oceaneering, Ecopetrol
Geothermal 6 5 Newmont, Schlumberger

Nuclear vocabulary is a term of art; the others are ESG boilerplate. An issuer writes "yellowcake" only if it handles the material. "Offshore wind" and "tidal energy" appear in every integrated oil company's sustainability section.

Other verticals need no lexicon because SIC already reaches them: storage (Fluence, Eos, Enovix, Amprius — all 3690), hydrogen (Plug, Bloom, FuelCell, Ballard — all 3620), geothermal (Ormat — 4911). Nuclear fuel was the only genuine structural gap: 0 of 8 names reachable.

Widening was tested and rejected:

variant 2023–26 Sharpe 2025–26 Sharpe
Nuclear only (shipped) 1.23 1.12
+ solar 0.90 1.01
+ wind 1.23 (no change) 1.12

Solar costs a third of the Sharpe. ARRY and NXT are genuinely asset-light so they rank as longs, but their revenue depends on subsidy and they have no pricing power. Capital intensity measures where capital sits, not whether revenue is durable — a real and unfixed limitation of the signal. Wind changes nothing: no US-listed pure-play wind manufacturer clears the liquidity floor (BWEN too small, TPIC delisted).

8. What electricity/energy-market knowledge is actually load-bearing

Honest accounting, because most of it turned out not to be:

Load-bearing: - Merit-order economics — knowing who funds capacity and who is paid by it. This is the entire thesis and it is domain knowledge, not a screen someone would reach by accident. - SEC classification of the fuel cycle. Knowing that uranium miners file under generic mining codes is why the universe is complete. No amount of statistics recovers it. - Fuel-cycle vocabulary. Knowing "yellowcake" and "uranium concentrates" are terms of art while "green hydrogen" is boilerplate. - Intermittent vs firm capacity. Knowing why solar suppliers are structurally different from switchgear suppliers explains §7's failure rather than leaving it a mystery.

Not load-bearing — tested and failed (see docs/power-market-signal-root-cause-results.md): - ISO/LMP prices, congestion, load forecasts, generation mix, capacity auctions, storage levels, spark spreads, weather. ~42 attempts. The mechanism: energy futures markets efficiently price public power fundamentals, and equities price off those futures, so public ISO data reaches equities already impounded. - Grid-stress indices specifically: a rolling grid-stress beta inverts — by 2025–26 it ranks intermittent solar as most stress-exposed and uranium/merchant generation as least.

The usable conclusion: power-market knowledge pays in classification, not in timing. It tells you which companies belong in the universe and why the categories differ. It does not tell you when to be long.

9. Known weaknesses

  1. Cycle dependence. In-sample Sharpe 0.41. Kill condition: light-minus-heavy basket spread flat for four consecutive quarters → stand down.
  2. Metric selection used ex-post labels. Eleven metrics screened against a hand-classified list; capex intensity won at t = −15.1 vs −7.2 next best. The stated rule needs no labels, but the choice was outcome-informed. Only forward OOS resolves.
  3. Factor overlap. Related to documented asset-light/quality factors. Untested against a generic version outside this sector.
  4. Borrow. Utilities are borrowable; EOSE/SLDP/SERV/NRGV are not. Assume the short book is partially unfillable.
  5. Survivorship — unfixed. 2,168 of 2,535 filers in these SIC codes have no current listing; yfinance serves no delisted prices. Long legs flattered, short legs understated. Affects the shipped book equally. Needs CRSP or equivalent.
  6. Correlation 0.78 to the shipped book. These are not independent allocations.

10. Full specification — step by step

Everything needed to rebuild the strategy from nothing. Each step names the module and function that implements it.

Step 1 — Resolve ticker → CIK

edgar.ticker_to_cik() → https://www.sec.gov/files/company_tickers.json (~10,459 filers), cached as data/cache/ticker_cik.json.

Trap: SEC returns HTTP 403 unless the User-Agent carries a contact address, and www.sec.gov/files/* additionally rejects an explicit Accept: application/json (while efts.sec.gov requires it). _headers(accept_json=False) handles the split. Both cost a silent failure when first built.

Step 2 — Enumerate the structural universe

edgar.sic_population(sic) → browse-edgar?action=getcompany&SIC=…&type=10-K&output=atom, paginated 100 per page to a 900 cap, parsed with <company-info[^>]*> → <cik>. Run for every code in data/sic_codes.csv.

Trap: the tag carries attributes, so a bare <company-info> regex matches nothing, and EDGAR emits mangled ARRAY(0x…) company names — take the CIK, ignore the name.

Codes, and the three deliberate exclusions, are listed in §1. Yield: 2,535 filers, 367 currently listed.

Step 3 — Discover the fuel cycle by language

universe.discover_lexicon_members(fetch, cik_to_ticker, years). For each universe-year Y, query efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index with forms=10-K,40-F,20-F and startdt=Y-2-01-01, enddt=Y-1-12-31 — strictly before Y begins — for each phrase in config.FUEL_CYCLE_LEXICON. Union the CIKs, map to tickers, record the year. A name stays eligible from its first qualifying year onward.

fetch_ciks is injected rather than imported, so this is testable with no network.

Step 4 — Price and liquidity

Adjusted closes and volume for every candidate plus benchmarks. Require MIN_PRICE_HISTORY_DAYS = 400. universe.liquidity_mask() computes a 252-day rolling median dollar volume (min 120 periods), resampled to month-end, and requires > $10M. The universe therefore breathes: 100 names (2019) → 131 (2023) → 159 (2025).

Step 5 — Pull point-in-time fundamentals

edgar.company_facts(cik) → data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK##########.json. Keep only us-gaap USD facts from 10-Q/10-K with all of start, end, filed, and a span of 60–200 days.

item tag preference order
revenue Revenues → RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax → …IncludingAssessedTax → SalesRevenueNet
capex PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment

First tag with data wins (break), so a filer reporting both Revenues and the ASC-606 tag is not double-counted.

Why the 60–200 day span filter: XBRL mixes quarterly, year-to-date and annual facts under the same tag. Without it a trailing-four-quarter sum silently double-counts.

Step 6 — Build the TTM panel, point-in-time

fundamentals.ttm_panel(facts, dates). For each month-end d:

  1. visible = facts[facts.filed <= d] — the single most important line in the codebase.
  2. groupby(ticker, item, end).last() on filing order — restatements resolve to the most recently filed value as known at d.
  3. Take the last 4 quarter-ends by end and sum.
  4. Skip any ticker-item with fewer than 4 filed quarters — no partial year is annualised.

Step 7 — Score

fundamentals.capex_intensity(panel) → wide date × ticker frame of TTM capex ÷ TTM revenue, with revenue ≤ 0 mapped to NaN.

Trap: pivot_table drops all-NaN columns by default, so a name with unusable revenue would vanish silently instead of being excluded explicitly. dropna=False keeps it as a visible NaN column.

Step 8 — Eligibility mask

universe.membership() = liquidity mask AND lexicon effective-date mask. Structural (SIC) members always pass the lexicon leg; lexicon members pass only from their first qualifying year.

Step 9 — Target weights

backtest.target_weights(score, mask, quantile=0.25, short_weight=0.5):

eligible = score.where(mask)
ranks    = eligible.rank(axis=1, method="first")
n        = eligible.notna().sum(axis=1)
n_pick   = max(1, round(n * 0.25))   if n >= 2 else 0
longs    = ranks <= n_pick
shorts   = ranks >  n - n_pick
weights  = longs/‖longs‖ − shorts/‖shorts‖ × 0.5

Trap — this one is load-bearing. Selecting by percentile (rank(pct=True)) with the default average tie handling can empty a leg entirely: eight tied names in a 13-name pool all land at pct 0.35 and none clear a 0.25 cut, while the short bucket fills normally. Counting with method="first" guarantees both legs are populated and raised realised breadth from ~15/16 to 22/22 names.

Long leg is fully invested; short leg is scaled to 0.5×. The book is net long by design.

Step 10 — Returns, costs, volatility target

backtest.run(weights, returns):

  1. Hold weights between rebalances, apply with a one-day lag (held.shift(1)).
  2. Charge |Δweight| × 10bp.
  3. Scale by min(20% / trailing-42-day realised vol, 1.5), lagged one day.
  4. Charge the leverage change itself — vol targeting is not free.

API note: cost and vol_target take None to mean use the config default. Pass 0 to disable. vol_target=None silently enables 20% targeting — this cost a confusing debugging session and now has a regression test.

Step 11 — Reporting

backtest.summarise() uses the repo-standard grid_equipment_basket.backtest.compute_metrics at RF = 4%, so every figure is comparable with the shipped book. backtest.diagnostics() reports average long/short breadth and annualised turnover.
